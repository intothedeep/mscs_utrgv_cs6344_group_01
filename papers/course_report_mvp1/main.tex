\begin{abstract}
% src: structure from the owner (data, prior work, why, result, future work), 2026-11-18; sentences from paper Sections 1, 2 and 7 (D-052)
The Polymarket-v1 database records every trade on Polymarket from 2022 to 2026.
Studies of horse racing and earlier prediction markets find that cheap bets win less often than priced.
Readers take a Polymarket price as a probability, so we test whether it is one.
On \NMarketsKept{} resolved Yes/No markets, we ask three questions: is the crowd right, who is the crowd, and what do trades look like?
Prices are not calibrated as a whole, but the gap is small and runs one way: events happen slightly less often than priced.
A few wallets hold most of the trading volume and take most of the winnings.
Trade sizes are strongly right skewed, so the median describes a typical trade better than the mean.
Future work can correct prices after the fact and test the correction on later markets.
\end{abstract}

\hd{1. Data and method}
We use the Polymarket-v1 database, which holds \PaperTrades{} trades and \PaperMarkets{} markets from \PaperPeriod{} \citep{qin2026polymarketv1}.
Of \NMarketsInScope{} Yes/No markets in scope we keep \NMarketsKept{} (\PctMarketsKept).
We read each market's price \DaysShort{} day before its scheduled close. A price after the close can contain the answer.
We drop prices older than \MaxStaleHours{} hours and markets with fewer than \MinTrades{} trades.
We cut the price into \NBins{} equal-width bins.
We test each bin with a binomial test (Benjamini-Hochberg corrected \citep{benjamini1995controlling}).
We test all bins with a chi-square test.
We fixed the criteria in a pre-registration before any Q1 result.
We report one later rule change as an amendment (tie rule for the price).

\hd{5. Limitations}
\textbf{Independence.} Markets on one event move together, so the p-values may be too small. No event key exists, so we size this only with proxy keys.
\textbf{Selection.} Only \PctMarketsKept{} of markets pass the filters. Kept markets trade more than dropped ones.
