Largest implied gap 2.22% against the 1.57% line
Source: paper Section 5.1, Appendix G (M5)
Speaker: Q1 speaker. About 1 minute 15 seconds. Why: with this many markets, a tiny gap gives a tiny p-value, so a small p alone does not tell us whether the gap matters. How: we look at size three ways. The largest per-bin gap, a practical line, and plain accuracy. The practical line is the mean half spread of 1.57%, fixed before the run. The half spread is skewed: its median is only 0.50%, so the mean line is a high bar and a typical market costs less to trade. Result: the largest per-bin gap is 3.29 points. The largest implied gap is 2.22%, above the line as a point estimate. But a price passes only when its whole 95% band lies beyond the line, and 0 of 7 grid prices do. Accuracy at the 0.5 line is 83.4% against 63.4% for always guessing the majority. We set no size line before the results, so small is our reading. Meaning: the gap is real in direction but we cannot say it is surely larger than the mean half spread.
