Q3: why the shape matters, and how we look at it

Why: with a long tail, the mean misleads. The shape tells us which summary to trust.

Count the values in bins and see the shape.

Illustrative values: trades of 1, 2, 3 and 1,000 USDC. The mean is 251.5, the median is 2.5.
Source: paper Section 4.4, Section 5.3
Speaker: Q3 speaker. About 1 minute. Why: a mean can mislead when a few values are huge, so we check the shape before we pick a summary. How: step one is a histogram. Step two is a QQ plot against the normal, where points on a straight line mean close to normal. Step three repeats the QQ plot on log values. Step four fits a normal, an exponential and a power law and scores each with the KS statistic. Here is a tiny example with illustrative values. Four trades of 1, 2, 3 and 1,000 USDC have a mean of 251.5 but a median of 2.5. The mean describes none of the trades. Result and meaning: the next slides use our real data.
