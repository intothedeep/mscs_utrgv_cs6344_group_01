Three questions, one reason: can we trust the price?

Q1
Is the crowd right?
Do events priced at 70% happen about 70% of the time?
Matters: a price we can trust is a probability.
→
Q2
Who is the crowd?
Does a small group of wallets do most of the trading?
Matters: it shows who the crowd is. Volume is not price-setting power.
→
Q3
What does betting look like?
How are trade sizes, wallet volumes and prices spread?
Matters: the shape tells us which summary number is honest.
Source: README section 1, paper Section 1
Speaker: Intro (Taek Lim). About 45 seconds. Why: the hook gave us one question, so we split it into three. How: each question gets the same four steps. Why we did it, how we did it, what we got, and why it matters. Result: Q1 asks whether the crowd is right, meaning whether events priced at 70% happen about 70% of the time. Q2 asks who the crowd is, meaning whether a small group of wallets does most of the trading. Q3 describes what the betting looks like, meaning how trade sizes, wallet volumes and prices are spread. Meaning: Q1 tests the price directly. Q2 tells us who stands behind it, but volume is not price-setting power. Q3 tells us which summary number is honest.
