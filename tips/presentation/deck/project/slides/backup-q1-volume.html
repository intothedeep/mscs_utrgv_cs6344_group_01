Q: Does the gap depend on trading volume?

Volume: USDC traded in a market up to 1 day before close, the time we read the price.
Groups: all 47,948 markets, cut into 3 equal groups (about 16,000 each) at the 1/3 and 2/3 points. Fixed before the run.
Only the busiest third is near 0. High minus low: +0.71 points (p = 0.046), a weak difference. The middle third has the largest gap.
Group labels in USDC. Source: paper Appendix G, peer question a; run 150310 peer_a_results.json
Backup, Q1 speaker. About 45 seconds. Why: a classmate asked whether the gap depends on how much a market trades. How: volume is the USDC traded in a market up to one day before the close, the same time we read the price. We cut all 47,948 markets into three equal groups at the one-third and two-thirds points, 499 and 6,207 USDC. We fixed this split before the run. In each group we redo the Q1 test. Result: the low group has a net gap of minus 1.12%, the middle group minus 1.43%, and the high group minus 0.42%. Only the high group's interval reaches zero. High minus low is plus 0.71 points with p equal to 0.046. Meaning: the busiest markets are close to calibrated. But the evidence is weak, and the gap does not shrink step by step, because the middle group has the largest gap. Volume also counts money, not people, so a few large wallets can make a market look busy.
