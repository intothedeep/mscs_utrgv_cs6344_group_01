Q1 how: bin the prices, compare, then test

1 Bin the prices
10 bins: 0 to 10%, 10 to 20%, up to 90 to 100%.
→
2 Compare
Observed rate of outcome 1 against the mean price, bin by bin.

3 Chi-square test
Goodness of fit on the 10 bins. The rule was fixed before the results.
→
4 Per-bin tests
Binomial test in each bin, with Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) correction.
Example: the 70 to 80% bin holds 2,301 markets. Mean price 74.6%, observed rate 71.3%.
Source: paper Section 4.2, Table 1
Speaker: Q1 speaker. About 1 minute 15 seconds. Why: we need a test that compares the price with what happened, and a rule we cannot bend after seeing the answer. How: step one splits the prices into 10 bins. Step two compares the observed rate of outcome 1 with the mean price in each bin. Step three is the chi-square goodness of fit test from Lecture 12. It decides the verdict as a whole, and we fixed this rule before we saw any result. Step four runs a binomial test in each bin with Benjamini-Hochberg correction, because ten tests at once need a correction. Take the 70 to 80% bin as an example. It holds 2,301 markets, the mean price is 74.6%, and the observed rate is 71.3%. Result: the next slides show it. Meaning: the order protects the verdict, because the rule came before the data.
