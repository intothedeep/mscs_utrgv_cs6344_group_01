Source: paper, Data section; Qin and Yang 2026 (Polymarket-v1)
Speaker 1 (Taek Lim). About 1 minute. Why: to test a price, each market needs a clear answer, a price one day before the close, and a crowd behind that price. Each rule removes markets that miss one of these. How: we use Polymarket-v1, a public database of 1.2 billion trades and 1.3 million markets from November 2022 to April 2026. In scope are 688,131 standard yes/no markets. Each arrow shows what one rule removes from the bar above it. First, 255,420 Up/Down markets. The dataset paper says they settle a different way, so we do not mix them in. Next, 62,718 with no clear winner, so there is no outcome to compare. Next, 215,289 with no price one day before the close. A later price can already contain the answer, so without that price there is nothing to test. Next, 80,230 whose price is older than 24 hours, so it misses the news since then. Last, 26,526 with fewer than 10 trades. Their price comes from one or two people, not a crowd. We fixed these rules before we saw any Q1 result. Result: 47,948 markets are left, 7.0% of the scope. Meaning: all three questions use the same 47,948 markets, so the three answers fit together. The results hold for markets that pass these filters.
