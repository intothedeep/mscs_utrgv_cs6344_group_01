Right 98% of the time is not the same as a true probability

Winner calling

Question: did the favorite win?

Score: how many races were called right.

Calibration

Question: do events priced at 70% happen 70% of the time?

Score: do the priced chances match how often events happen.

Illustrative values: 100 races priced at 90%. The favorite wins all 100, so it is 100% right. A calibrated 90% should lose about 10, so this price is not calibrated.
Our data: right 83.4% of the time at the 0.5 line, yet the chi-square test says the prices are not calibrated as a whole.
Speaker: Intro (Taek Lim). About 1 minute. Why: a high hit rate sounds like a good probability, but these are two different questions. How: winner calling asks whether the favorite won. Calibration asks whether events priced at 70% happen about 70% of the time. Here is a tiny example with illustrative values. Take 100 races priced at 90%. If the favorite wins all 100, the hit rate is 100%. But a calibrated 90% should lose about 10 of them, so these prices were not calibrated. Result: in our own data the markets are right 83.4% of the time at the 0.5 line, yet the chi-square test says the prices are not calibrated as a whole. Meaning: being right often does not make a price a true probability. That is the gap our project tests.
