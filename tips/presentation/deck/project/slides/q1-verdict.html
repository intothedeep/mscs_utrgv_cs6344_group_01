Q1 result: prices are not calibrated as a whole

Left: points sit just under the diagonal. Right: 9 of 10 bins below 0
χ² = 68.0
chi-square goodness of fit, 10 bins, 47,948 markets
p = 1.1×10⁻¹⁰
assumes independent markets, so it may be too small
x axis: mean price of the markets in each bin, 1 day before close. Source: paper Section 5.1
Speaker: Q1 speaker. About 1 minute 15 seconds. Why: this is the test we fixed before the results, so its answer is the Q1 verdict. How: it compares the observed counts in the 10 bins with the counts the prices predict. Result: the chi-square statistic is 68.0 with 10 degrees of freedom, and p is about 1.1 times ten to the minus ten. The verdict is that prices are not calibrated as a whole. In both panels the x axis is the mean price of the markets in each bin, one day before close. On the left, the points sit just under the diagonal, so events happen a little less often than priced. On the right, we zoom in on that distance: the gap is the observed rate minus the mean price. Nine of the ten bins are below zero. The p-value assumes independent markets, so it may be too small. Meaning: the prices do not match what happened, and the next slide shows how and by how much.
