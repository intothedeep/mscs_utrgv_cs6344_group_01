Q1 meaning: prices run a little high, in one direction

What for
χ² says the prices miss. The bin tests ask where, and which way.
Result
9 of 10 bins below the price. Bonferroni keeps 4 bins, BH keeps 5, all below. Largest gap 3.29 points: priced 74.6%, happened 71.3%.

Why it matters
Right 83.4% of the time is not calibrated. Read a price as a probability, a little lower.
Dots: gap (rate − price) per bin. Dashed: favorite-longshot shape only. Source: paper Section 5.1; run 142082
Speaker: Q1 speaker. About 1 minute 15 seconds. What for: the chi-square test said the prices miss, 68.0 against a cut of 18.3. But it does not say where or which way, so we tested each bin. Result: 9 of 10 bins are below the price. After correcting for ten tests, Bonferroni keeps 4 bins and Benjamini-Hochberg keeps 5, and all of them are below the price. The largest gap is in the 70 to 80% bin: prices average 74.6%, but 71.3% of those events happen. Meaning: outcome 1, which is mostly Yes, is priced a little high at almost every price. In horse racing there is the favorite-longshot bias: cheap bets win less often than priced, and favorites win more often. The dashed line shows that shape. Only our two cheapest bins match it. At high prices our gaps are also below zero, the opposite of that bias. So our pattern is a small shift down almost everywhere. We do not test why. Why it matters: a market can be right 83.4% of the time and still not be calibrated. You can read a Polymarket price as a probability, but read it a little lower.
