Q2: who is the crowd? We measure how concentrated volume is

Why: a price is the crowd’s price, so we ask who the crowd is. How: four steps.

By USDC volume, makers and takers apart.

4 Lorenz curve
Volume share against wallet share, next to the 80/20 line. Outside the lectures.
Source: paper Section 4.3, Section 5.2
Speaker: Q2 speaker. About 1 minute 15 seconds. Why: a price is the crowd’s price, so before we trust it we ask who the crowd is. Does a small group of wallets do most of the trading. How: step one ranks the wallets by their volume in USDC, a dollar-pegged token, and we do this for makers and takers apart. Step two measures the share of volume held by the top 1% and the top 20% of wallets, and compares it with the 80/20 rule, which says the top 20% hold 80%. Step three draws the complementary cumulative distribution function, CCDF, on log axes. Step four draws the Lorenz curve, which is outside the lectures and which the paper labels as such. Result: the next slide. Meaning: a wallet is an address, not a person, so we describe concentration of volume and do not test who sets the prices.
