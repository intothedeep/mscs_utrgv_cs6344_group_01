Goal: keep the expected share of false findings among the findings under 5%.
1. Sort the 10 p-values from small to large.
2. Rank i gets the cut i × 0.05 / 10 = i × 0.005.
3. Find the largest rank under its cut (rank 5). Reject every bin up to it.
5 bins pass: the Bonferroni four and bin 8 (0.018 < 0.025). BH is our main rule, Bonferroni the stricter check.

Backup, Q1 speaker. About 40 seconds. Benjamini-Hochberg, or BH, guards a different thing: the expected share of false findings among the bins we flag. We sort the ten p-values. The bin at rank i gets the cut i times 0.005, so the cut grows with the rank. We find the largest rank whose p-value is under its cut. That is rank 5, bin 8, with 0.018 under 0.025. Rank 6, bin 3, misses its cut, 0.058 against 0.030. So we reject every bin up to rank 5: five bins. BH is our main rule, and Bonferroni is the stricter check. Only bin 8 differs between them.
